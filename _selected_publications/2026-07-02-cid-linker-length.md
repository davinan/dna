We introduce a strategy for coupling synthetic chemically induced dimerization (CID) systems to functional readouts by engineering the linker-mediated coupling interface. Using single-fluorescent-protein sensors built around circularly permuted GFP, we mapped how paired N- and C-terminal linker lengths determine CID-to-output coupling across nanobody-, monobody-, and de novo-designed CID architectures, yielding sensors with dynamic ranges up to 1270% and robust responses in mammalian cells.
